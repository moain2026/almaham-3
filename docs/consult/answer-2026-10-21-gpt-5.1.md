**(d) Type classifier → fixed integer cell count per type (top choice)**

Rationale:

- Your failure cases are *per-type consistent*:
  - CRYSTAL: typically 2–4 integer, then 2 decimals, then “kWh”.
  - EMETER_GREEN: 6 + 2 decimals.
  - HPL 3‑phase: 5 int + 1 dec + unit.
- Once you know the type, the integer/decimal split is essentially *deterministic* and cheap:
  - Example: `if type == CRYSTAL: int_len = 3 (or max non-blank before unit)` etc.

Minimal implementation:

- **Step 1 — Crop-type classifier**
  - Input: the **existing line crop** (or slightly larger LCD crop) you already extract for the reader.
  - Model: tiny CNN (e.g. 4 conv blocks + 1 FC) or reuse the **same backbone as digit reader** with a parallel classification head.
  - Classes: {TOMAS_SMART, TOMAS_STATIC, TAGADOD, EMETER_GREEN, CRYSTAL, HPL, CHINT, DDS, OTHER}.
  - Params: you can stay under ~50–80k additional params; negligible runtime.
  - Train easily on 500+ images with one label per image; you’re growing ~100/day so type labels are cheap.

- **Step 2 — Per‑type fixed integer count**
  - For simple meters: “6 integer, ignore rest”.
  - For CRYSTAL-like: rules such as:
    - Ignore blanks on left.
    - Count contiguous digit cells until either a known unit glyph (“kWh”, “W”) box begins, or you hit the type’s *max integer cells*; everything after that is decimal/garbage.
  - Use your digit boxes from reader; sorter already exists.

Why this beats others:

- Doesn’t depend on the tiny ~1px dot at all.
- Robust against same‑height fractions.
- Robust to faint LCDs if the overall type is still visually clear (housing, label color, surround).
- Low complexity; easy to mirror in Kotlin/Python.

Trade‑offs:

- Requires you to **reliably recognize meter type** even from partial crops. For CHINT/HPL, body color and label shape are strong cues; this is very solvable with ~500–1k samples.
- Mis-typing a meter → systematic off‑by‑N digits. So you must:
  - Have a **“unknown/uncertain” type** class with high rejection threshold.
  - Only enforce per‑type integer count when type probability is high, else fall back to other heuristics (see 2nd choice).

**(c) Per-cell integer/decimal classifier using position-from-right + neighbors**

As a **backup on top of (d)** or when type is uncertain.

Implementation:

- For each digit cell (from your reader’s per-digit boxes), compute:
  - Normalized x position (e.g. rank from left / from right).
  - Normalized width/height.
  - Distance to nearest **unit text** region (kWh/W) if you detect it.
- Two variants:

  1. **Image-based micro-classifier per cell:**
     - Crop each cell at higher zoom (e.g. 32×32 grayscale).
     - Tiny CNN (or even MLP on features from the reader backbone) outputs P(integer) vs P(decimal).
     - Optionally use a small 1D context (e.g. look at 3-cell strip) to recognize the *pattern* “...digit digit unit-text”.

  2. **Feature-based classifier:**
     - Tag presence of unit glyph on the right via another small classifier on the right of the line crop.
     - Use hand-crafted features: “cells to the left of first unit glyph are integer; right side are decimal/ignored”.

Why it’s good:

- Uses cues that are much more robust than the dot:
  - Position from right.
  - Proximity to units text.
- Works even when you mis-type meter (mitigates issue (d) has).
- Training: label integer/decimal for each box from the *known ground-truth integer length* for that sample; no extra annotation cost.

Recommended combo:

- Use **(d) as primary**: type → fixed int count.  
- Use **(c) as override** for:
  - Type=UNKNOWN or low-confidence type.
  - Or where predicted integer boxes conflict with obvious unit glyph on right.

**(b) Sequence head (CTC with boundary token)**

In principle strong (like CRNN for text), but **overkill and fragile here**:

- To work, you need a reasonably powerful sequence encoder (BiLSTM or 1D conv stack) plus CTC decoding.
- You’re constrained to ONNX Runtime minimal ops; adding RNN ops or additional heavy layers costs size and engineering time.
- With **~500 labeled line images**, CTC models often:
  - Overfit common patterns (“XXXX.XX”) and still mislocate boundaries on rare views.
  - Are brittle to irregular spacing and units glued to digits.
- Also, you already have a per-digit locator; you’d effectively be duplicating functionality.

I would **not prioritize** CTC now. It’s a later-stage alternative if you ever refactor to one model for “read full line as text sequence” after you have thousands of labels and can justify more ops.

**(a) Extra heatmap class “last integer digit” supervised from text length**

I would **not do this**; here’s why:

- Supervision is *weak and noisy*:

**(b) “Display” class on same locator net – but with structured use**

Instead of a generic “display” class only in CenterNet, I’d structure:

- Keep CenterNet-lite backbone (213k params).
- Add two heads:
  1. **LCD head**:
     - Predicts bounding box (or heatmap center + width/height) for the main display area.
     - Supervision from annotated LCD boxes (you can label quickly).
  2. **Line head**:
     - Predicts main-reading-line center + size, **constrained during inference** to favor positions lying inside or just above/below the LCD box.

Inference logic:

1. Predict top N LCD boxes (often N=1).
2. Predict candidate line boxes.
3. Penalize line candidates whose IoU with LCD box is below a threshold or whose vertical position is inconsistent (e.g. top-right of LCD with units more likely small fragments, not the main row).
4. If serial numbers/blue labels are fully outside predicted LCD, they’re hard-suppressed.

Why this works:

- Serial labels/blue strips are generally **outside** the LCD bounds.
- Top-right partial-digit+“kWh” artifacts are **sub-components** inside LCD but:
  - Smaller height than the main digit row.
  - Lower aspect ratio or inconsistent vertical alignment across digits.

Param / runtime:

- Reusing the same backbone; additional head is just a few conv layers → <50k params extra.
- Still well under your 300k parameter budget.

You do need:

- ~200–300 manually annotated LCD boxes (not big; you can label corners/boxes easily in a day).

3. Confidence / rejection for CenterNet + rule assembler

Goal: nearly no **confident-wrong**; accept only when confident.

I’d use **multi-signal, calibrated** confidence:

1. **Digit-level signals**
2. **Sequence-level aggregation**
3. **Stability under augmentation**
4. **Temporal consistency (if previous reading known)**

Then calibrate using a **small learned combiner** and a held-out set.

---

**Signals to compute**

Let each predicted reading have N integer digits.

Per-digit:

- p_i = max softmax prob at digit i.
- s_i = heatmap peak (CenterNet score) for digit i.
- h_i = normalized digit height (as fraction of line box height).
- g_i = local gap metrics, etc. (optional).

Sequence-level:

- MinDigitProb = min_i p_i.
- MeanDigitProb = (1/N) Σ p_i.
- MinHeatmapScore = min_i s_i.
- LineScore = CenterNet line detection score (from locator).
- TypeConf = probability of meter type (if you add type classifier).
- BoundaryConsistency = binary/score measure whether boundary logic (type-based integer length, etc.) is satisfied cleanly (no conflicting decimals, no misaligned units).

Augmentation consistency:

- Run **TTA** with 2–4 light variants:
  - Rotations: −5°, +5°.
  - Slight scale jitter: ±5–10%.
- For each aug, compute reading string; then:
  - TTA_Agree = fraction of TTA variants equal to original reading.
  - TTA_EditDist = average Levenshtein distance to original.

Temporal/checksum consistency:

- If previous confirmed reading R_prev exists:
  - Δ = R_curr − R_prev.
  - Domain rule: Yemens meters rarely go backward; typical daily increment range.
  - TemporalScore = clamp/transformation, e.g. exp(−|Δ − μ| / σ), where μ, σ estimated from your field.

---

**How to combine & calibrate with ~100 held-out photos**

I’d avoid heavy models; use a small logistic calibrator:

1. Construct a **validation set** (separate from training; 100–200 real images).
2. For each prediction:
   - Compute vector F = [MinDigitProb, MeanDigitProb, MinHeatmapScore, LineScore, N, TTA_Agree, TTA_EditDist, BoundaryConsistencyFlag, TypeConf, TemporalScore (if available)].
   - Label y = 1 if prediction exactly matches GT, else 0.
3. Fit **logistic regression** (L2-regularized) on F → y.
   - This is fast on CPU, can be done offline in Python.
4. Use the logistic output as **CalibratedConfidence** in [0,1].

At runtime:

- Compute F in Kotlin/Python.
- Evaluate **precomputed weights** from logistic regression (just dot product + sigmoid).
- Accept reading if CalibratedConfidence ≥ τ, where τ chosen from validation to get target false-positive rate:
  - E.g. choose τ such that FPR < 1% and coverage is still acceptable.

Why not just “min digit peak” or product?

- Product of probabilities tends to be extremely small and not linearly related to actual correctness.
- MinDigitProb alone misses:
  - Cases where all digits individually look good but the **line localization is wrong** (A,D,E).
  - Cases where boundary logic is wrong (B,C,F).
- TTA + boundary consistency + line score *together* catch those structural errors.

**I would do:**

- Combine:
  - MinDigitProb
  - LineScore
  - TTA_Agree
  - BoundaryConsistency
  - Maybe TemporalScore when available
- Learn a **1-layer logistic** calibrator.
- Do not bother with a dedicated learned CNN head just to predict confidence; you already have all relevant signals.

4. Training recipe on CPU with ~500 real + synthetic

What moves the needle most under this setup:

1. **Balanced sampling / loss weighting for rare types**
2. **Better negative mining (hard distractors)**
3. **EMA (Exponential Moving Average) of weights**
4. **Curriculum / staged training**
5. **Careful synthetic generation to cover type specifics**

---

**(1) Loss weighting / sampling**

Your most critical types (Crystal, EMeter, HPL, CHINT) are underperforming. You must:

- Maintain a **per-type sampling schedule**:
  - In each batch, enforce minimum quota of rare types: e.g. 50% of line crops from “difficult” types until they reach acceptable performance.
- Or weight their losses higher:
  - For each sample with type t, multiply loss by w_t ≈ (1 / freq_t)^α with α ~ 0.5–0.7, clipped to avoid huge weights.

This is **low-cost, high-gain**.

---

**(2) Hard negative mining**

For **locator** in particular:

- Build a pool of **hard negatives**:
  - Serial numbers, blue labels, top-right LCD areas, button labels, random text within meter image.
- With your user rectangle as input, randomly crop:
  - Slightly different windows that contain serial labels or other printed digits but *not* the main line.
- Train locator:
  - Either with explicit negative points (no peaks) in those regions.
  - Or, if using anchor-free CenterNet style, ensure all peaks outside GT get strong negative gradients.

Given your self-training trap (only training on what locator already finds), I’d:

- Incorporate your **text-supervised localization** outputs (sliding reader) to add correct positives for images the locator currently misses.
- Then mine false positives from the same images as negatives.

This likely gives >10–20% boost in locator robustness on full photos.

---

**(3) EMA (Exponential Moving Average)**

EMA of weights usually gives more stable predictions with little cost:

- Maintain θ_EMA = α θ_EMA + (1−α) θ, with α ~ 0.99–0.999.
- Use θ_EMA for validation and final export.
- Especially helpful in small-data regimes to reduce variance.

CPU cost: essentially trivial.

---

**(4) Curriculum / staged training**

Given synthetic + real:

- Stage 1:
  - Train on mixed **synthetic + easy real** (clear TOMAS, TAGADOD) to convergence.
  - Focus: stabilize digit shapes and basic localization.
- Stage 2:
  - Freeze some early layers (or reduce LR) and **oversample difficult real types** (Crystal, EMeter, HPL, CHINT).
  - Optionally reduce augmentation strength to not obliterate the already weak signals (faint LCD etc.).
- Stage 3:
  - Fine-tune with **hard negatives** for locator and decimals/units for reader.

Do **not** overdo curriculum complexity (no 5-phase schedules); 2–3 well-defined phases suffice.

---

**(5) Synthetic generation**

Your synthetic seven-seg scenes currently don’t model:

- EMETER_GREEN’s green PCB background.
- Crystal’s “same height decimals + almost invisible dot + kWh right after”.
- HPL 3‑phase style (units glued right, wide LCD band).

You should:

- Build **type-specific synthetic generators**:
  - EMETER: 6+2 digits, same height, faint dot (alpha-blended).
  - CRYSTAL: 2–4+2 digits, same height, faint / missing dot, “kWh” text using similar font and spacing.
  - HPL: 5+1 + “W” unit.
- Overlay on cluttered / tinted backgrounds approximating real glass, glare bands, scribbles.

But: do not spend weeks; a basic scripting pass can be built in 1–2 days and gives non-trivial gains, especially for unit text and tight decimals.

---

**I would NOT focus on:**

- Fancy losses (TTFNet Gaussian tweaks, focal-loss variants, etc.) beyond your existing setup; they’re minor compared to the data/balancing issues.
- AutoML-style hyperparameter search; you don’t have data or compute budget.
- Pseudo-labeling loops driven by your current weak locator & reader *before* fixing the self-training bias; you’d reinforce existing biases.
